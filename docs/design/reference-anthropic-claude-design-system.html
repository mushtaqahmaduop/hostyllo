Anthropic · Claude

Design system teardown · every layer

A field journal on warm parchment, with one clay accent reserved for action.

Ivory and oat neutrals replace the cool grays every other AI product uses. A custom serif carries body copy at 20px — unusual for tech, and the single loudest signal in the system. Elevation comes from surface tone and hairlines, never shadow. There are no gradients.

Serif for voice.
Sans for chrome.

1 · The gradient question, answered first

You asked what colour gradient the platform uses. The honest answer: none. No linear-gradient, no radial glow, no colour wash, no mesh, no glassmorphism, no shadow-based elevation anywhere in the core system.

What replaces gradient is a tonal ladder — four flat ivory values stepped a few percent apart. Depth is read from the tone shift plus a 1px hairline, not from light falloff. If you copy one thing from this system, copy that.

The ladder that stands in for elevation

3 · Typography

Three custom faces, self-hosted and renamed at the CSS layer: Anthropic Serif, Anthropic Sans, Anthropic Mono. Their glyph lineage is Tiempos / Copernicus (Klim) and Styrene B (Commercial Type). The official fallback chains are famously short — Georgia, Arial, Noto Sans.

The defining move: body copy is set in the serif at 20px. Almost no other tech product does this. Sans is demoted to chrome — nav, buttons, labels, badges. Get that split wrong and nothing else will read as Anthropic.

4 · Hierarchy — how importance is signalled

Rank order of the devices this system uses to say "this matters more", strongest first:

#DeviceMechanic
1Type size + face68px serif → 24px sans → 20px serif → 12px sans. Size does most of the work.
2Surface tone stepcanvas → card → feature. A brighter or warmer fill = more important.
3Whitespace80–120px section gaps. Isolation signals rank.
4Text colour ramp#141413 → #3d3d3a → #6c6a64 → #b0aea5. Four steps, no more.
5Hairline rules1px #cccbc8. Groups content; never decorates.
6The clay accentLast resort, one per page. Colour means "act", not "look".
Primary / secondary / tertiary in this system are tonal, not chromatic. Primary action = filled ivory or clay. Secondary = 1px outlined. Tertiary = bare underlined text. There is no blue, no purple, no second brand colour to reach for.

Look at the ivory button's corners: bottom-only 8px radius, top corners sharp. It reads like a tab pulled from a stack rather than a pill. That single asymmetry is the most recognisable component detail in the whole system.

5 · Motion, hover, and micro-interaction

Motion is restrained to the point of being nearly invisible. There are no parallax effects, no scroll-jacking, no card lift on hover, no glow, no spring physics, no "after effects" in the compositing sense. Everything is a short colour or opacity transition.

7 · The rules that hold it together

Do

  • Set body copy in the serif at 20px; keep sans at 12–16px for chrome.
  • Use canvas #f0eee6 with cards at #faf9f5; reach for manilla only for a true feature.
  • Keep the bottom-only 8px radius on filled buttons — it's the signature.
  • Spend the clay accent on exactly one action per page.
  • Keep inline link underlines permanently visible.
  • Use 24px radius on every card-level surface.

Don't

  • Don't introduce cool greys, blues, or anything outside the warm earth family.
  • Don't use box-shadow for elevation — step the surface tone instead.
  • Don't add gradients, glows, or colour washes. Flat solid fills only.
  • Don't set body text in sans. Sans is chrome.
  • Don't use pure white #ffffff as a surface — it breaks the paper metaphor.
  • Don't apply the accent to icons, hovers, or decoration.
